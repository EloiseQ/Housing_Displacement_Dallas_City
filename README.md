#### Displacement Risk Analysis in Dallas County
##### Introduction
- Gentrification and rising housing costs are reshaping Dallas County, putting thousands of long-time residents at risk of displacement. A recent report found that one in five Dallas neighborhoods is in the early stages of gentrification, leading to increased property values and rent hikes that disproportionately impact low-income communities (Dallas News, 2024). This shift is particularly concerning for renters, who lack the stability of homeownership and are most vulnerable to eviction. As luxury developments replace affordable housing, many working-class families, particularly Black and Hispanic residents, are being priced out of their own neighborhoods (NBC DFW, 2024). The affordability crisis is expected to worsen, with projections indicating that by 2032, the typical Dallas renter will only be able to afford 21% of available rental units, making displacement an increasingly urgent issue (Hockaday Forecast, 2024). 
This study assesses displacement risk in Dallas County by integrating U.S. Census data with eviction filings to identify populations most vulnerable to housing instability. The key factors considered in this analysis include eviction rate, which measures the proportion of eviction filings relative to the total population, homeownership risk, which reflects the percentage of non-homeowners as an inverse indicator of housing stability, and rent burden, which captures the share of renters allocating more than 30% of their income to rent. By aggregating these variables, a displacement risk measure is constructed to evaluate which demographic groups face the highest risk. The analysis focuses on disparities between renters and homeowners, racial and ethnic disparities, the effects on children and seniors, and the relationships between key displacement indicators. The objective of this study is to provide data-driven insights to inform housing policy interventions aimed at mitigating displacement.
